Morphing hydrogels have versatile applications in soft robotics, flexible 
electronics, and biomedical devices. Controlling component distribution and 
internal stress within a hydrogel is crucial for shape-changing. However, 
existing gradient structures of hydrogels are usually non-reconstructable, 
once encoded by chemical reactions and covalent bonds. Fabricating hydro-
gels with distinct gradient structures is inevitable for every new configuration, 
resulting in poor reusability, adaptability, and sustainability that are dis-
advantageous for diverse applications. Herein, a hydrogel containing revers-
ible photo-crosslinks that enable reprogramming of the gradient structures 
and 3D deformations into various configurations is reported. The hydrogel 
is prepared by micellar polymerization of hydrophobic coumarin monomer 
and hydrophilic acrylic acid. The presence of hexadecyltrimethylammonium 
chloride micelles increases the local concentration of coumarin units and 
also improves the mechanical properties of the hydrogel by forming robust 
polyelectrolyte/surfactant complexes that serve as the physical crosslinks. 
High-efficiency photodimerization and photocleavage reactions of coumarins 
are realized under 365 and 254 nm light irradiation, respectively, affording 
reversible tuning of the network structure of the hydrogel. Through photo-
lithography, different gradient structures are sequentially patterned in one 
hydrogel that direct the deformations into distinct configurations. Such a 
strategy should be applicable for other photolabile hydrogels toward repro-
grammable control of network structures and versatile functions.

1. Introduction
Inspired by the widespread morphing structures in nature, 
various self-shaping materials have been developed due to their 
versatile applications in flexible electronics, soft actuators, and 
biomedical engineering.

bonds, especially the photolabile ones that can be locally 
tailored by photolithography. [8]  The morphing hydrogels with 
dynamic covalent networks intrinsically have better stability 
than the ones based on gradient physical associations or 
photoisomerization.
[9]  Recently, photolabile covalent bonds 
such as disulfides with dynamic exchange reaction, trithiocar -
bonates with reshuffling reaction, and anthracenes with rever -
sible cycloaddition reaction, are incorporated into polymers, 
elastomers, or hydrogels to afford tailored network structure, 
self-healing, and shape memory properties.
[10]  Among these 
photolabile groups, coumarin and its derivatives have been 
extensively studied due to the high-efficiency dimerization 
and cleavage reactions (without the need of photosentizers or 
catalysts), excellent biological activities and biocompatibility, 
and tunable fluorescent properties.
[11]  Coumarins dimerize 
under 365  nm light irradiation to form a cyclobutane ring by 
the [2  + 2] cycloaddition, which can be cleaved under 254  nm 
light irradiation to revert to the original structure via photos-
cission reaction.
[12]  Incorporating coumarin units into gel 
matrix should enable the reversible formation of additional 
crosslinks to spatially tune the network structure. However, to 
reprogram the gradient structures and the morphing process 
of one hydrogel to form distinct configurations, there are still 
two issues to be addressed: i) high-efficiency photodimerization 
and photocleavage of coumarins within a hydrogel should be 
guaranteed, especially when the concentration of coumarins 
is much lower than that in polymers and elastomers;
[12c,13]  
ii) the swollen regions with photocleaved coumarin dimers 
can be deswelled to the original dimensions for restoring the 
network structure and next-round encoding of structural gra-
dient via photodimerization and photocleavage of coumarins, 
respectively. For the first issue, coumarin units can be locally 
enriched in hydrogels by microphase segregation. For example, 
coumarin-containing triblock copolymers self-organize into 
nano-sized micellar aggregates that should favor the photodi-
merization of coumarins.
[14]  However, this effect has not been 
well exploited, and the research is limited in weak hydrogels. 
About the second issue, the hydrogel with coumarin dimers 
as the crosslinks will swell after the photocleavage reaction,
[15]  
which hardly recovers its original dimensions due to the dif -
ferent network structure and swelling pressure. To solve this 
problem, another kind of dynamic crosslinks should be incor -
porated, which are weak in a certain condition to manifest the 
influence of coumarins at different states, yet robust in another 
condition to reduce the influence on swelling and mechanical 
performances of the gel.
Herein, we report a robust hydrogel with repeatedly tunable 
network that enables reprogramming of gradient structures 
for morphing into distinct 3D configurations. The hydrogel is 
prepared by micellar copolymerization of coumarin acrylate 
and acrylic acid in the aqueous solution of hexadecyltrimethyl-
ammonium chloride (CTAC) micelles. After equilibrium in 
water, the micelle structure is stabilized by forming poly-
electrolyte/surfactant complexes (PESCs) between the weakly 
charged poly(acrylic acid) (PAAc) and the opposite charged 
CTAC.
[16]  The formation of PESCs not only increases the local 
concentration of coumarins that affords high-efficiency photo-
dimerization,
[17]  but also acts as the physical crosslinks that  
improve the mechanical properties of the hydrogel. [18]  Thanks 
to the pH-responsiveness of PESC hydrogel and the reversible 
photodimerization and photocleavage of coumarins, gradient 
structures are reconstructable in one hydrogel by photoli-
thography to program the shape change and form distinct 3D 
configurations. We believe that such hydrogels with reprogram-
mable network structures will expand the applications of gel 
materials in engineering and biomedical fields.
2. Results and Discussion
The photolabile monomer, 7-(2-methacryloyloxyethoxy)-
4-methylcoumarin (MAEMC), is synthesized according to 
the protocol described in Supporting Information. As shown 
in Figure 1, a small fraction of the hydrophobic MAEMC is 
incorporated into the hydrophilic PAAc network by thermal-
initiated micellar copolymerization,
[19]  followed by irradiation 
under 365 nm light to trigger the [2  + 2] cycloaddition of cou-
marins inside the CTAC micelles. The coumarin dimers act as 
additional chemical crosslinks of the gel matrix. The concen-
trations of AAc, MAEMC, and CTAC are set as 4 m, 0.08 m  
(2 mol% of AAc), and 0.3 m, respectively, because of the best 
performances, as discussed in the following section. The 
resultant gel is regarded as the as-prepared hydrogel, which is 
immersed in water to remove the residuals and reach the equi-
librium state. During this process, PAAc chains partially ionize 
due to the increase in pH, and form robust PESCs with the 
oppositely charged CTACs, as confirmed by Fourier transform 
infrared spectra (Figure S1, Supporting Information).
[18a,20]  
Therefore, the equilibrated hydrogel is dually crosslinked by 
coumarin dimers and nano-sized PESCs with average size 
of ≈15  nm, as revealed by transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM) observation (Figure S2, Supporting Information). How-
ever, after the hydrogel is irradiated under 254  nm light, the 
coumarin dimers are cleaved, leading to reduced crosslinking 
density and thus swelling of the hydrogel in water. The revers-
ible photodimerization and photocleavage reactions, as well as 
the tunable network and physical properties of the hydrogel, 
can be harnessed for reprogramming the gradient structures, 
the morphing process, and the final configurations.
Adsorption spectroscopy is applied to quantitatively monitor 
the photodimerization and photocleavage of coumarins within a 
hydrogel film after the micellar copolymerization. As shown in 
Figure 2a, a characteristic absorbance peak at 320 nm, which is 
ascribed to the coumarin double bond, gradually decreases upon 
365 nm light irradiation, indicating the dimerization to form a 
cyclobutane ring.
[21]  After subsequent irradiation under 254 nm 
light, the absorbance peak gradually increases, indicating the 
photoscission reaction of coumarin dimers (Figure  2b). The 
photodimerization and photocleavage of coumarin units can 
be reversibly triggered by alternating irradiation under 365 and 
254 nm light. The dimerization degree, D , can be calculated by

The mechanical properties of the hydrogels are examined 
by tensile tests. The as-prepared hydrogel with dimerized 
coumarins as additional chemical crosslinks shows moderate 
mechanical properties, with tensile breaking stress (σ
b) of 
110  kPa, breaking strain (ε b) of 155%, and Young’s modulus 
(E) of 120  kPa (Figure S3, Supporting Information). After 
being equilibrated in water, the photo-crosslinked hydrogel 
becomes more robust, with σ b of 200  kPa, ε b of 220%, and E 
of 160  kPa (Figure 3a,c and Figure S4, Supporting Informa-
tion), although the water content (q) and the swelling ratio in 
length (S) increase from 62 wt% and 1.0 to 80 wt% and 1.2, 
respectively. Such improved mechanical properties are related 
to the partial ionization of PAAc chains, favoring forming 
robust PESCs with the oppositely charged CTACs. After irra-
diation under 254 nm light for 30 min to cleave the coumarin 
dimers, the hydrogel becomes soft and swollen in water, with 
the increase in q and S to 2.3 and 96 wt%. Consequently, the 
photo-decrosslinked hydrogel is weakened, with σ
b of 35  kPa, 
εb of 140%, and E of 30 kPa. We should note that no CTACs are 
released out of the swollen gel because of the cooperative inter -
actions of the ionic bonding between the oppositely charged 
CTAC and PAAc and the hydrophobic association between 
the alkyl chain of CTAC. [16,18]  The hydrogel is also stable and 
possesses good mechanical properties in physiological saline 
(Figure S5, Supporting Information). The swollen and weak-
ened hydrogel with photocleaved crosslinks can be reverted to 
the robust one by two steps: i) the gel shrinks at a specific con-
dition (S = 0.9 when pH = 3), followed by high-efficiency photo-
dimerization of coumarins under 365  nm light irradiation; ii) 
the hydrogel with recovered network is equilibrated in water 
and exhibits similar swelling and mechanical performances

this regulation, the equilibrated hydrogels with dimerized

As shown in Figure  3c,d, the fluorescent spectrum of photo-

photo-crosslinked gel. The varied fluorescence is synchronized 
with the swelling behavior of the hydrogel and ascribed to the 
aggregation-caused quenching effect and also the different 
states of coumarins.
[12c,25]
The markedly different yet repeatedly tuned swelling and 
mechanical properties of the hydrogel should benefit from the 
formation of PESC that not only enables high-efficiency photo-
dimerization of coumarins with increased local concentration, 
but also enlarges the influence of different states of coumarins 
on the macroscopic performances of the gel. To verify this 
hypothesis, we examine the swelling and mechanical proper -
ties of gels with similar q after photodimerization of coumarins 
with and without micelles. To ensure similar network structure 
and easy-removing the micelles, the gels are prepared in the 
presence of sodium dodecylsulfate (SDS) micelles that cannot 
form PESCs with PAAc chains. To maintain the integrity 
of gel during the swelling process, a small amount of chem-
ical crosslinker is added to the precursor solution. As shown 
in Figure S6, Supporting Information, S of the equilibrated 
hydrogel after dialyzing out SDS is 3.7 . This swollen hydrogel 
is then converted to the original size by solvent evaporation, 
followed by a photodimerization step. The resultant hydrogel 
also becomes swollen in water (S  = 3.3) and mechanically 
weak. In contrast, the hydrogel experienced photodimerization 
in the presence of SDS micelles moderately swells in water, 
with S of 2.3. The different swelling capacity of the photo-
crosslinked hydrogels indicates that the presence of micelles 
favors high-efficiency photodimerization of coumarins. How-
ever, the equilibrated photo-crosslinked hydrogel prepared in 
the presence of SDS micelles is much weaker than that pre-
pared in the presence of CTAC micelles (Figure S7, Supporting 
Information). The swelling ratio of the former gel (S = 2.3) was 
also larger than the latter (S  = 1.2). This result manifests the 
contribution of PESC to the mechanical robustness of the gel.

To enable reversibly programming gradient structures 
and thus shape-morphing in one hydrogel, the deformed gel 
should be converted to the original state (uniform network 
and flat shape) at a certain condition for the next-round photo-
lithographic patterning. We found that incubating the gel strip 
(t
i,254  = 10 min) with θ of 40° in an acidic solution can eliminate 
the swelling mismatch between the photo-decrosslinked and 
photo-crosslinked regions. The deformed gel retains the bent 
shape with θ increasing from 34° to 49°, as pH increases 
from 5 to 8 (Figure  4c). However, the gel strip fully recovers 
the flat state in a bath with pH of 2–4. Considering the pK
a 
of PAAc (≈4.7), [26]  the deformation of the gel strip should 
be related to the ionization of PAAc. Too much ionization of 
PAAc is harmful to the formation of robust PESCs. When  
2 ≤ pH ≤ 4, the gel matrix is mainly crosslinked by PESCs, and 
the crosslinking function of coumarin dimers becomes negli-
gible, which eliminates the swelling mismatch between the 
photo-crosslinked and decrosslinked regions and flattens the 
gel strip to the original shape. In the solution with relatively 
high pH such as in water, the ionization produces exces-
sive electrostatic repulsion between PAAc chains, resulting in 
swelling of both photo-crosslinked and decrosslinked regions. 
However, the swelling ratios at different regions are influenced 
by the different states of coumarins. The swelling mismatch is 
enhanced with the increase in pH from 5 to 8, leading to an 
increased folding angle. Here, pH = 3 is chosen as the condition 
for unfolding the gel strip and also for mending the network

elimination of gradient structure and swelling mismatch. Such

The hydrogels with good mechanical properties and repro-
grammable deformations have broad applications in biomedical 
devices, flexible electronics, and soft actuators/robots. To simply 
demonstrate the potentials, we design a soft gripper by using 
this morphing hydrogel that can twine firmly around a glass 
rod and lift an agate ball (Figure S12, Supporting Information). 
Another example is using morphing hydrogel to control the 
light switch and the state of a lamp. UV light irradiation creates 
through-thickness gradient and results in bending deformation 
of the same gel strip, which pushes the ball to block the laser 
and switch on the lamp. The reprogramming ability of morphing 
hydrogels should open opportunities for diverse applications by 
affording reusability, sustainability, and adaptive functions.
3. Conclusions
Reprogrammable deformations have been realized in one 
single hydrogel based on reversible crosslinking and recon-
structable network. High-efficiency photodimerization and 
photocleavage reactions of coumarins are achieved by increased 
local concentration of coumarins inside CTAC micelles, which 
form PESCs with partially ionized PAAc matrix. The swelling 
and mechanical properties are regulated by the photolabile 
crosslinks that favor reprogramming the structural gradi-
ents and 3D deformations of the hydrogel. The presence of 
PESCs also renders the hydrogel with good mechanical per -
formances, as well as distinct yet controllable swelling capacity 
between the photo-crosslinked and photo-decrosslinked states. 
Besides coumarins, other dynamic covalent bonds including 
trithiocarbonates and anthracenes can be used to reprogram 
the network structure of gels and elastomers for specific  
functions and applications including soft robotics, flexible elec-
tronics, and tissue engineering.
